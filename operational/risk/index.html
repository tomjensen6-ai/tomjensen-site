Operational Risk Assessment

A practical method for identifying operational risk, assessing probability and impact, defining mitigation tasks and assigning clear ownership until completion.

Purpose

To show how operational risk can be made visible, prioritized, reduced and followed up through a simple controlled structure.

Risk assessment principle

Operational risk assessment should be simple enough to use, but clear enough to support decisions. The purpose is not to create paperwork. The purpose is to make risk visible before work begins or before an abnormal condition becomes worse.

A practical risk assessment should identify the risk, estimate probability and impact, define mitigation tasks, reassess the remaining risk after mitigation, and assign ownership until the action is completed.

Working principle: Identify risk → Assess probability and impact → Define mitigation → Reassess residual risk → Assign owner → Track completion.

Basic risk formula

A simple operational model can use:

Risk Score = Probability × Impact

In Norwegian operational language, probability is often described as sannsynlighet. The method is the same: estimate how likely the event is and how serious the consequence would be.

Suggested scoring model

The scale below is a practical example. Real organizations should adjust definitions to their own risk appetite, safety requirements, contractual commitments and operational environment.

Score Probability / Sannsynlighet Impact
1 Rare / unlikely under normal conditions Minor local effect, no critical impact
2 Possible but not expected Limited operational disturbance
3 Possible during the activity or condition Operational impact, reduced redundancy or service concern
4 Likely if no controls are applied Major operational impact or serious degradation
5 Very likely or already developing Critical impact, safety concern or major service loss

Risk level colors

Colors are useful because they make risk level easier to understand quickly. The color should support the decision — not replace the explanation.

Risk Score Level Color Typical Action
1–4 Low Green Proceed with normal controls and monitoring.
5–9 Medium Yellow / Amber Proceed only with defined controls and responsible owner.
10–16 High Red Requires mitigation, approval and clear escalation path.
17–25 Critical Dark Red Do not proceed without senior approval and risk reduction.

Stop conditions

A risk assessment should define when work must stop. Stop conditions protect the team from continuing simply because the activity has already started.

  • Actual conditions do not match the approved plan
  • Redundancy becomes uncertain
  • Critical alarm appears unexpectedly
  • Asset identity or system state cannot be confirmed
  • Rollback path is no longer available
  • Safety conditions change
  • Required personnel or monitoring are unavailable
  • Residual risk is higher than accepted

Stop rule: If the risk picture changes, stop and reassess before continuing.

Risk ownership

Risk ownership must be explicit. A mitigation task without an owner is only a suggestion.

  • Risk Owner: accountable for accepting or reducing the risk.
  • Action Owner: responsible for completing a mitigation task.
  • Technical Reviewer: confirms that controls are technically valid.
  • Operational Approver: confirms that remaining risk is acceptable for execution.

Risk should not be accepted by the person who simply wants the work completed. Acceptance should sit with the role responsible for the operational consequence.

Operational exposure

Operational exposure describes the period where the facility is more vulnerable than normal. This is often more important than the task duration itself.

Examples of operational exposure include:

  • Reduced power redundancy
  • Reduced cooling capacity
  • Temporary loss of monitoring visibility
  • Open panels, bypass states or maintenance modes
  • Restricted access routes
  • Delayed rollback capability
  • Increased dependency on human coordination

Residual risk

Residual risk is the risk remaining after mitigation tasks are applied. It should be reviewed before work starts or before an incident response is considered stable.

If residual risk remains high or critical, it should not be hidden by wording. It should be escalated to the correct authority for decision, acceptance, further mitigation or cancellation.

Core Rule

Risk assessment is only useful when mitigation tasks have owners and completion dates.

Color Rule

Colors help clarify risk level, but the decision must still be based on probability, impact, mitigation and residual risk.
