Data Hall Access Control

How I would handle access to a data hall. Most of the risk comes from people who are allowed in, not from strangers: the wrong rack, the wrong cable, work nobody approved.

Classification

An example I wrote for this site. A real procedure must follow the site's security policy, customer contracts and local rules.

Document ID: SOP-SEC-003
Revision: 2.0
Status: Example
Approval: Example only

1. Purpose

Every person who enters a data hall can affect the equipment in it. This procedure checks who goes in, why, where they may go and what they may do, and that everyone comes out again with the room as they found it.

2. The real risk

Strangers are the obvious risk, and the access system handles most of that. The bigger risk is approved people doing the wrong thing. A vendor pulls a cable in the wrong rack. A contractor starts work that was never approved. A visitor props a door open or leaves a floor tile up, and airflow and security suffer.

Packaging is a common one. Customers and vendors often carry boxes straight into the hall. Cardboard brings dust and fire load into the room, so unpacking happens outside the hall. Watch for it on every visit.

A door held open for a colleague lets in someone nobody checked.

Access approval lets someone into the room. Any work on equipment also needs a work approval, such as an approved change or work order. Without one, they may look, but not touch.

3. Before the visit

  • ☐ Access request approved: who, why, which room, which rack or cage, when
  • ☐ Work approval in place if any work will be done
  • ☐ Escort decided: needed or not, and who
  • ☐ Visitor has done the site induction, or will get the briefing at entry
  • ☐ No incident, test or maintenance in the room that should stop the visit

4. Who does what

Role Task
Requester (customer or vendor contact) Asks for access in advance: names, reason, room, time.
Security Checks identity and approval, issues and collects badges, logs entry and exit, watches doors and cameras.
Operator on duty Confirms nothing in the room should stop the visit. Tells the escort if conditions change.
Escort Stays with the visitor, makes sure the rules are followed, checks the area at exit.
Shift lead Approves exceptions, decides on visits during incidents or maintenance.
Facility manager Owns the access policy and reviews the logs.

Note: Whether a visitor needs an escort depends on two things: the site's security level and the work. On high-security sites, everyone is escorted all the time. Elsewhere, a trained vendor may enter alone for a simple task, but for work on critical equipment an operations person stays with them. Write the rules in the site's access policy.

Staffing:

  • No one on site: no visits. Emergency access by a vendor follows the site's own process, agreed in advance with the On-call person.
  • One person: alarms are watched all the time, whatever else happens. Whether that person can also escort is decided case by case. If the vendor is there to fix the alarm in that room, going with them may be the right call, as long as alarms are still seen. Otherwise a visit that needs an escort waits.
  • Two or more: one person escorts, the other stays on alarms and the phone.
  • Security: checks identity, issues badges and watches door alarms and cameras. Where Security is on site, they can escort if the site allows it.

5. Steps

Step Action Check Owner
1 Check the request: who, why, which room and rack, when. If work is planned, find the work approval. Request approved and matches the visit. Work approval found if needed. Security
2 Ask the Operator on duty if anything in the room should stop the visit. No incident, test or maintenance there, or the Shift lead approves. Security, Operator on duty
3 Check identity against the request. Photo ID matches the named person. Security
4 Issue a badge or card for the approved rooms and time only. Card opens only the approved doors, and expires with the visit. Security
5 Name the escort if one is needed. Escort present before entry. Shift lead
6 Brief the visitor: one person per badge swipe, doors closed, no photos unless approved, no work beyond the approval, no packaging in the hall, where the exit is, and what the fire alarm (and gas-extinguishing alarm, where the hall has one) sounds like. Visitor confirms the rules. Escort or Security
7 Hold point: no entry until steps 1–6 are done. All checks done. Security
8 Enter through the approved door. Each person's entry logged. Escort
9 Stay with the visitor during the visit. Work only on approved equipment. No packaging brought in. Doors closed. Floor tiles in place. Escort
10 Check the area before leaving. Doors closed, tiles in place, nothing left behind, no packaging. Escort
11 Log the exit. Everyone who went in has come out. Security
12 Collect the badge, or disable the temporary card. Badge returned or card disabled. Security
13 Record anything unusual. Each finding recorded, with an owner. Security, Shift lead

6. If it goes wrong

Someone tailgates, or there is a person in the hall you don't recognize: don't confront them if it feels unsafe. Call Security. Security identifies them and escorts them out. Record it, and check what they had access to.

A visitor works outside the approval or on the wrong equipment: stop the work at once, calmly. Call the Shift lead. Check the equipment and alarms before anything else is touched.

An alarm or incident starts during the visit: pause the visit. The escort brings the visitors out of the hall or to a safe place, and Security uses the log to check everyone is accounted for.

Fire alarm, or gas-extinguishing alarm where the hall has one: leave at once by the nearest exit. The escort counts their visitors at the assembly point. See Fire Alarm Response (EOP-FIR-004).

Lost badge: disable it at once and record it.

Access given by mistake: get the person out safely, disable the card, tell the Shift lead and record it. Check what they did while inside.

7. After the visit

  • ☐ Everyone out, and the count matches the entry log
  • ☐ Badges returned or cards disabled
  • ☐ Area checked: doors closed, tiles in place, no packaging
  • ☐ Anything unusual recorded and reported

8. Rules and standards

ISO/IEC 27001 includes controls for physical access to secure areas. EN 50600-2-5 covers security systems for data centers. Customer contracts often add their own rules for their space. If the site stores visitor logs or camera footage, these are personal data; in Europe GDPR applies, so keep only what is needed, for no longer than needed.
