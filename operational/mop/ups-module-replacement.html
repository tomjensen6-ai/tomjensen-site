UPS Module Replacement

How I would replace a power module in a modular UPS. The vendor does the work on the module. Operations owns the UPS, the load and the decision to go on at each hold point.

Classification

An example I wrote for this site. A real procedure must follow the manufacturer’s instructions, the site’s electrical safety rules and its own change process.

Document ID: MOP-UPS-004
Revision: 1.0
Status: Example
Approval: Example only

1. Purpose

Replace one power module in a modular UPS while the load stays on the UPS, then return the UPS to normal operation with its redundancy back.

2. The real risk

This work usually starts with an alarm. One module has shut down, and the others have taken its load. Nothing is down, and it is easy to treat it as a routine part swap. It isn’t. The UPS has already lost a module’s worth of redundancy, and it stays that way until the new module is in and working.

If the modules that are left can’t carry the load, the UPS transfers to static bypass. The load then runs on mains with no UPS protection. A mains disturbance during that time reaches the IT equipment directly.

Taking the wrong module offline is the second danger. It removes a working module from a UPS that is already short of one. That can be enough to send the UPS to bypass.

The new module can be a risk of its own. The frame accepts it and checks it automatically, but that only tells you it fits. If the module carries a firmware version that has never run on this site, you are testing it on a live load. Ask the vendor which version it has before it goes in.

The person doing the work faces real hazards too. Modules are heavy, often too heavy for one person. Capacitors inside can hold charge after the module comes out. The DC side stays live as long as batteries are connected, and there is an arc flash risk at the slot.

A planned swap, for example after a service notice from the manufacturer, follows the same steps. The difference is that you start with full redundancy and can choose the time.

Note: Many modular UPSs allow a module to be replaced while the UPS carries the load. Some designs, or some faults, need the UPS in maintenance bypass first. The manufacturer’s procedure decides. If bypass is needed, follow UPS Maintenance Bypass (MOP-UPS-001) and treat the load as unprotected for the whole window.

3. Before you start

  • ☐ Change approved and window agreed
  • ☐ Fault logs read: which module, what failed, any other alarms
  • ☐ Load checked against what the remaining modules can carry, with margin
  • ☐ The other supply path (B side) normal, with no work planned on it
  • ☐ Replacement module on site: right part number, undamaged
  • ☐ Firmware version of the new module known and compared with the modules in the frame
  • ☐ Manufacturer’s procedure on site and read
  • ☐ Lifting aid on site if the module needs one
  • ☐ PPE for the work and the arc flash risk at the UPS
  • ☐ Alarms and thresholds for the UPS visible in BMS/EPMS
  • ☐ Way back agreed: what happens if the new module won’t come online

4. Who does what

Role Does
Work lead (named in the plan) Runs the work, holds the briefing, decides at each hold point. Can be one of the two operations people below.
UPS vendor specialist Takes the module offline, replaces it and brings the new one online, following the manufacturer’s procedure. Works under the vendor’s own electrical authorization.
Operator on duty, at the UPS Stays with the vendor. Reads the UPS display and confirms the module and each step.
Second person on BMS (Operator on duty or Shift lead) Watches alarms and thresholds in BMS/EPMS for the UPS, the room and the other supply path. Reports any change at once.
Shift lead Decides if the work stops or the window is extended. Escalates.
Change manager Owns the change record and closes it.

Staffing:

  • No one on site: the work does not start. A failed module found remotely is reported to the On-call person, who decides how fast the replacement must happen.
  • One person from operations on site: the work does not start. One person can’t stay with the vendor and watch BMS at the same time.
  • Two or more from operations on site: one stays with the vendor at the UPS. The other watches BMS and is free to act if something changes.
  • Security: escorts the vendor and checks that access is approved. Access approval does not approve the work.

5. Steps

Step Action Check Owner
1 Hold the briefing: the work, roles, hold points, stop rule and way back. Everyone knows who can stop the work. Work lead
2 Check the UPS: on inverter, alarms, load per module, battery state. Load can be carried by the remaining modules with margin. Operator on duty, at the UPS
3 Check the other supply path in BMS. B side normal, no work on it. Second person on BMS
4 Identify the module to replace: module tag, slot number and serial against the fault log and work order. Mark it. Vendor and Operator on duty agree it is the right module. UPS vendor specialist
5 Hold point: The Work lead confirms the load, the other path and the right module before anything is taken offline. Go given before step 6. Work lead
6 Take the module offline, following the manufacturer’s procedure. Remaining modules share the load. UPS stays on inverter, no transfer to bypass. UPS vendor specialist
7 Wait the time the manufacturer sets, then remove the module. Use the lifting aid. Slot left safe as the manufacturer requires. UPS vendor specialist
8 Check the new module: part number, serial, damage and firmware version. Firmware matches the frame, or a newer version is approved by the vendor for this frame and accepted by the Work lead. UPS vendor specialist
9 Hold point: The Work lead confirms the new module and its firmware before it goes in. Go given before step 10. Work lead
10 Insert the new module, secure it and bring it online, following the manufacturer’s procedure. Module recognized and sharing the load. No new alarms. UPS vendor specialist
11 Watch the UPS for the time set in the plan. Even load sharing, normal temperatures, no alarms or thresholds crossed. Second person on BMS
12 Confirm the UPS is back to normal: on inverter, all modules online, redundancy restored, BMS shows the right state. Matches the state before the fault. Operator on duty, at the UPS
13 Hold point: The Work lead confirms the UPS is back to normal before the work is closed. Go given to close the work. Work lead
14 Put the module tag on the new module. Record the fault, the work and both serial numbers against the module tags. Tag the faulty module for return. Records complete, change closed. Change manager

Note: Module weight, wait times, how a module is taken offline and whether the empty slot needs a cover all vary between manufacturers and models. The manufacturer’s procedure decides each one. When each module has its own tag, you can follow its faults and service history, which matters when one module is newer than the rest.

6. If it goes wrong

If the UPS transfers to bypass, or anyone is unsure about the load, stop. Take nothing else offline.

The UPS goes to bypass during the work: the load is on mains without protection. Stop the work. The vendor and the Shift lead decide how to get the UPS back on inverter. The Shift lead escalates while the load is on bypass.

Mains fails during the work: follow Utility Power Loss Response (EOP-PWR-001).

The load is lost on this path: the other path carries the IT equipment that has dual supplies. Equipment with a single supply goes down. The Shift lead escalates at once and follows the site’s own plan for informing customers.

The new module won’t come online or shows a fault: take it out again. The UPS runs with reduced redundancy, as it did before the work. This stays escalated until a working module is in. Only put the old module back if it was removed for a planned swap and is known to work.

The new module comes online but behaves differently from the others (uneven load sharing, alarms, high temperature): take it offline again and handle it as a module that won’t come online.

The wrong module was taken offline: bring it back online at once if the manufacturer’s procedure allows it. Stop the work and check the UPS state before anything else.

Smoke, a burning smell or a fire alarm: follow Fire Alarm Response (EOP-FIR-004).

Someone is hurt: people first. Call for help and follow the site’s own plan.

7. After the work

  • ☐ UPS on inverter, all modules online
  • ☐ Redundancy back to the design level
  • ☐ No alarms active or suppressed
  • ☐ BMS/EPMS shows the right state
  • ☐ New module tagged, firmware version recorded
  • ☐ Faulty module tagged and packed for return
  • ☐ Serial numbers updated in the maintenance system
  • ☐ Area clean, covers and doors closed
  • ☐ Change closed

8. Rules and standards

The IEC 62040 series covers UPS systems: part 1 for safety, part 3 for performance. Work on and near electrical installations follows the local rules: EN 50110-1 in Europe, FSE in Norway, the Electricity at Work Regulations 1989 in the UK and NFPA 70E in the US. The manufacturer’s instructions set how a module is taken out, replaced and brought back.
