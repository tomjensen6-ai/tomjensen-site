Operational Library / MOP

Method of Procedure Examples

Examples for planned work on power and cooling. Work where a mistake can take down load, so the order of steps, the hold points and the way back are agreed before you start.

Purpose

Planned work written down and approved before the window, not worked out during it.

What a MOP is for

A MOP covers planned work that can affect the load: maintenance, tests and changes. It is written for one job and approved before the work starts.

It says who does what, where the work stops for a go, and how to get back if it goes wrong. How changes are classified is on the Frameworks page.

Examples

MOP-UPS-001

UPS Transfer to Maintenance Bypass

Move the load from the UPS to maintenance bypass so the UPS can be serviced, and back again, without dropping the load.

MOP-UPS-004

UPS Module Replacement

The vendor swaps the module. Operations owns the UPS, the load and the go at each hold point.

MOP-PWR-002

Planned Utility Transfer Test

Move the site from utility to generators and back, on a day you chose, to find weak spots before a real outage does.

MOP-CLG-003

CRAC/CRAH Planned Maintenance

Take a cooling unit out for service and bring it back, while operations owns the room.

How the examples are built

The newer MOPs here follow the same pattern: the real risk, what must be in place before you start, who does what, the steps with hold points, how to get back if it goes wrong, and what to check afterwards.

A hold point is a step where one named person confirms the state before the next step can start. Stopped work restarts only after everyone has read the procedure through again and signed off.

The blank MOP template (Word) uses the same pattern.
