Fire Alarm Response

An example emergency procedure for the first minutes after a fire alarm in a data center. People first, then the source.

Classification

An example I wrote for this site. A real procedure must follow the site's fire plan, the fire system design and local fire rules.

Document ID: EOP-FIR-004
Revision: 1.2
Status: Example
Activation: Fire alarm, pre-alarm or smoke report

1. Purpose

What to do in the first minutes after a fire alarm: protect people, make sure the fire service is alerted, find the source, and keep control of the site.

2. Scope

Fire alarms, smoke and heat detection, aspirating smoke detection, suppression pre-alarms and discharges, and reports of smoke or a burning smell in technical rooms and support areas.

It does not replace the site's evacuation plan, fire plan or suppression system procedures.

3. When to use it

  • Fire alarm or pre-alarm on the fire panel
  • Smoke, heat or aspirating detector alarm
  • Suppression pre-alarm or discharge
  • Someone reports smoke, fire or a burning smell
  • Repeated or unexplained detector faults in a critical area

Rule: treat every alarm as real until you have checked it.

4. Safety rules

  • Never enter a room in suppression pre-alarm or after a discharge. Wait until the fire service or an authorised person has cleared the room and it has been ventilated.
  • Never go in alone. Check in pairs, with a radio or phone.
  • Feel the door with the back of your hand before you open it. If it is hot, or you see smoke, do not open it. Leave and report.
  • Do not silence, reset or isolate fire or suppression systems unless the site procedure allows it.
  • Do not use water on live electrical equipment.
  • Treat battery rooms as high risk. UPS batteries, especially lithium-ion, can re-ignite and give off toxic gas.

5. Staffing on site

Staffing differs between sites. Some have no one on site at night, some one person, some two or more. Two people at night is recommended for exactly this kind of situation, but it depends on cost and customer requirements. Whatever the staffing, personal safety comes first.

  • No one on site: the alarm goes to the alarm centre and the fire service. The on-call person is called out and meets the fire service. No one enters before the fire service has checked the area.
  • One person on site: do steps 1 to 3 first. Check only from outside the room: panel, BMS, cameras, the door. Do not go in. You lead until someone takes over. Call in the on-call person and meet the fire service.
  • Two or more on site: two people can check the area together, if the site plan allows it. If there are more, one stays at the panel, leads and meets the fire service.
  • Security on site: many sites have security staff around the clock. They often handle evacuation, the headcount, the gate for the fire service and keys. Agree in advance who does what.

6. First steps

Step Action Check Owner
1 Read the fire panel: zone, detector, alarm or pre-alarm. Location known. Operator on duty
2 Start evacuation if the alarm or the site plan calls for it. People leaving by the planned routes. Operator on duty / Security
3 Make sure the fire service is alerted. Check that the alarm has reached the alarm centre. If it has not, or you are unsure, call 110. Fire service alerted. Time logged. Operator on duty
4 The incident lead named in the site's fire plan takes charge. If you are alone, you lead until someone takes over. Everyone knows who is in charge. Incident lead from the fire plan
5 Count people at the assembly point against the list of everyone on site: staff, contractors and visitors. Everyone accounted for, or missing people reported to the incident lead at once. Security at night. In the daytime, a person the shift lead appoints.
6 Check the area as section 5 describes for your staffing. Follow the safety rules. Findings reported to the incident lead. Operator(s) on duty
7 Meet the fire service at the arrival point. Tell them first whether everyone is out. Then give the zone, keys or access cards, and known hazards: suppression, batteries, high voltage. Fire service briefed on arrival. Assigned person
8 Check power, cooling and IT impact in BMS/DCIM. Make no changes unless the incident lead and the fire service agree. Impact known and logged. Facility operations
9 Start the incident log: times, alarms, decisions, who was told. Log running. Assigned recorder
10 Inform stakeholders as the communication plan says. Set the time for the next update. Updates sent and logged. Incident lead

Headcount: The list of everyone on site must be ready before an alarm, not made during one. Keep it printed, or on a device that works without the building network, taken from access control and the visitor log. In large buildings an exact count is hard, so the list matters even more. If anyone is missing, tell the fire service at once.

Note: Some sites use a short check time before the alarm goes to the alarm centre, mainly for early warning detection such as aspirating smoke detection. It only works if there are enough people on site to check in time. Where the risk is high, it is often better to send the alarm straight away and accept some false call-outs. If false alarms happen often, find and fix the cause. If there is any sign of fire, call 110 at once.

7. Escalate at once if

  • You find smoke, fire, heat or a burning smell
  • Suppression is in pre-alarm or has discharged
  • Several zones are in alarm
  • There are signs of electrical overheating or arcing
  • Anyone is missing or hurt
  • The source cannot be checked safely
  • Power or cooling is affected

8. Updates

One person leads. If possible, someone else handles communication.

Keep updates short, factual and timed: Time — zone — status — what we know — impact — next update.

9. Back to normal

Only when the fire service, if it came, has handed the site back, and:

  • the cause is found, or the alarm is confirmed false and explained
  • the panel and detectors are back to normal; detectors exposed to smoke are cleaned or replaced
  • after a discharge, the room is ventilated and cleared, and the suppression system is refilled and re-armed by the service company. Until then, keep detection working, put out extra portable extinguishers in addition to the fixed ones, and follow the site's rule for running without suppression.
  • power and cooling are checked
  • the incident report is written, with what we learned
