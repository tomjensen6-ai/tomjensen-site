Operational Library / EOP

Emergency Operating Procedure Examples

Examples for the first minutes and hours of an emergency: power, cooling, water and fire.

Purpose

Get control first. The repair comes later, under its own procedure.

What an EOP is for

An EOP is for when the site is no longer in a known, normal state. It covers the first part: keep people safe, find out what is affected, stop it getting worse, call the right people and log what happens.

An EOP is not a repair manual. The repair follows its own MOP or the vendor’s procedure.

One person leads and keeps the overview. Others do the checks, the calls and the log. Log the time of every step: the log is where the review afterwards starts.

Examples

EOP-PWR-001

Utility Power Loss

What to do in the first hours after the utility supply is lost. People first, then battery, cooling and fuel.

EOP-CLG-002

Cooling Failure Response

When cooling is lost or temperatures start to rise. People first, then the cooling state, then the clock.

EOP-WTR-003

Water Leak Response

Water where it shouldn’t be. Find the source and stop the water without stopping the cooling by mistake.

EOP-FIR-004

Fire Alarm Response

What to do in the first minutes after a fire alarm. People first, then the source.

How the examples are built

The newer EOPs here follow the same pattern: when to use it, who does what, the first steps, when to escalate, and how to get back to normal. Every one starts with people first.

The blank EOP template (Word) uses the same pattern.
